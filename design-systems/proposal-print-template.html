Section 3

The three exports, and why these three

A general purpose export button produces a spreadsheet nobody wants. These three are shaped around things you already do, using the words your own files use.

1. The Allan Equipment order sheet

Because you do not manufacture or ship, a great deal of what you do is telling Allan Equipment what to build and where it goes. This export produces exactly that, in the shape of your own spring 2023 fulfilment workbook, so that Trent and Danie can work from it without translating anything.

Each row carries: farm name and contact, planter make and number of rows, kit type, power plug style with the part number written out as RE37651 for round or RE67013 for flat, quantity, sensor pack row units, condition, and current shipping state using your own wording, including Delivered to AEML, Not yet but paid for, and Has full kit now.

Why it earns its place: it removes a retyping job that currently happens every time an order goes across, and retyping is where plug styles get mixed up.

2. The printable call sheet

You are not always at a desk. This turns any filtered list into a document you can print and put on the passenger seat, or open on a phone in somebody's yard with no signal.

Each row carries: farm name, the right person to ask for, telephone number, region, what they already own, days since anyone last spoke to them, and a blank column wide enough to write in.

Why it earns its place: the upgrade list is only worth something once it leaves the screen. Filter to press wheel owners without Precise Product Placement, print it, and that is a week of calls in your hand.

3. The consent evidence record

Under Canadian anti spam law, consent attaches to a person, and the burden of showing you had it falls on the sender. Today that evidence is scattered across four files, and for fifty six people it cannot be connected to anybody at all.

Each row carries: the person, their farm, consent status, the date recorded, and the original file the consent came from.

Why it earns its place: it is the document you would want to be able to produce if you were ever asked, and producing it takes a moment rather than a fortnight.

A note on ordinary exports. A plain export of any filtered view to Excel is listed in section 4 as a separate addition at $150. The three above are different in kind, because each one is built around a specific job rather than dumping a table. If a plain export is all you want, that costs less.

Section 6

Twelve months of support, and what the five changes mean

Support runs for twelve months from the date the system is delivered. It divides into two categories, and the distinction matters, so it is set out precisely.

Unlimited and uncounted

None of the following draws on your allowance, however often it happens.

  • Anything broken. Errors, failures, and behaviour that does not match what was described to you
  • Questions, by telephone or email, as many as you wish to ask
  • Help when a spreadsheet you are importing behaves unexpectedly
  • Problems with accounts, hosting, certificates or your domain
  • Security updates and routine maintenance of the software the system depends on

Five changes across the year

Small additions and edits, each amounting to roughly half a day of work.

  • A new field on a farm, a person or a deal
  • A new filter, a new sort order, or a new dashboard panel
  • An additional column on any table
  • New values in a dropdown, new categories, or new pipeline stages
  • Changes to wording, labels or colour
  • An adjustment to the layout of any screen

What the five changes do not cover. Work amounting to a new screen or a new module, a connection to another system, a migration from a further data source, sending text messages, or anything requiring the database structure to be redesigned. Those are projects in their own right and are quoted before any work begins.

How this will actually go. If you ask for something and I believe it falls outside the five, I will tell you before doing the work, together with what it would cost. You then decide. You will never receive an invoice for something you did not agree to in advance.

Two further points. A change is yours to spend as you see fit; there is no approval process and no form to complete. Changes not used within the twelve months do not carry forward.
